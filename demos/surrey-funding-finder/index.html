Why it matters

How this helps councils and communities

Most funding information lives in PDFs, council newsletters, and fragmented government portals. Eligible organisations miss out because the process is too hard to navigate. This proof-of-concept explores what a plain-English, people-centred service could look like.

01

Plain English over jargon

Funding guidance is often written for administrators, not applicants. A well-designed service translates eligibility criteria into language that residents and small organisations can actually use.

02

One place, not many portals

Relevant funding sits across county council websites, national government platforms, lottery portals, and charitable foundations. A unified service reduces the time spent searching and the opportunities missed.

03

Matched to need, not category

Search by what you are trying to do, not by which funding body runs the scheme. A good service surfaces relevant opportunities that applicants would not have found by browsing categories alone.

04

Prepared applicants, stronger bids

Knowing what documents are needed, who an organisation needs to be, and what the funder is actually looking for produces better applications and fewer failed bids.

05

Shortlisting without spreadsheets

Community groups and small charities rarely have a grants manager. A simple save-and-shortlist feature replaces the personal tracking that currently happens in email inboxes and sticky notes.

06

Evidence for local government

Search data and shortlisting behaviour shows which funding needs are unmet, which communities are searching most, and where guidance gaps exist. That data is genuinely useful for commissioning and policy.

Why Not On Mondays built this

Good public services are built around people, not processes.

We built this proof-of-concept to show what a funding discovery service could feel like when it is designed for the person applying, rather than for the organisation administering it. No jargon, no dead ends, no "call us for more information."

  • Service design for local government and the public sector
  • Plain-English content and information architecture
  • Accessible digital products that meet WCAG standards
  • Discovery, prototyping, and user research
  • Delivery support for complex change programmes
"Residents are not failing to apply for funding because they are not trying. They are failing because the information is not designed to be found."
