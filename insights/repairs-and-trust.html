Repairs are rarely
the whole problem

In housing, residents often lose trust long before a complaint is filed.

May 2026 · 5 min read · Housing · Operations
Share · X · LinkedIn ·

On this page

  • The pattern
  • Two moments
  • What the data shows

Residents usually know whether a repair will be fixed. What they often do not know is what happens next, who is responsible, and whether anyone is watching.

Key points

  • Most resident complaints about repairs are not really about the repair itself.
  • Trust breaks when no one updates, when ownership is unclear, when residents have to chase.
  • The Housing Ombudsman upheld 73% of property condition complaints in 2024/25. The pattern is widespread.

The pattern

What the resident experiences What the organisation records
"Nobody updated me" Job logged correctly
"I keep repeating myself" Case transferred
"No one owns this" Awaiting contractor response
"I had to call six times" Six contact attempts recorded
"They knew about this for weeks" Follow-up scheduled

The records are accurate. The process was followed. And the resident still feels abandoned. That gap is not a communications failure. It is an operational one.

How a repair becomes a complaint

  1. Resident reports issue
  2. Repairs team logs ticket
  3. Contractor assigned
  4. Housing officer unaware handoff without notification
  5. Resident chases no proactive contact in 18 days
  6. Complaint created
  7. Trust collapses

Two moments

01

Uncertainty

Repair ticket: resident view
Ref REP-2024-8847
Issue Boiler, no hot water
Reported 3 Oct
Status In progress
Last updated 22 days ago
Next contact Not scheduled

A repair is reported. A ticket is raised. From that point, most residents receive nothing.

What they experience is silence. And silence, when your home is affected, reads as absence.

Residents do not need constant updates. They need to know someone is watching.
02

Ownership

Who owns this? Internal view
Repairs team Job assigned to contractor No further action expected
Contractor Access not confirmed Waiting on housing officer
Housing officer No referral received Unaware of open job

When a repair crosses teams or contractors, ownership rarely transfers cleanly.

The resident is not in any of those systems. They are in their home, waiting for a call that none of the three parties knows they should make.

Handoffs without clear ownership create gaps that residents end up filling themselves.

What the data shows

Volume

7,082

Formal determinations made in 2024/25, up 30% on the previous year.

Housing Ombudsman, Annual Complaints Review 2024-25

A growing caseload

The increase reflects more residents escalating complaints, and improvements in the Ombudsman's own capacity to process cases.

Repairs upheld

73%

Of property condition complaints were upheld. The category includes repairs, leaks, damp and heating failures.

Housing Ombudsman, Annual Complaints Review 2024-25

Found in the resident's favour

Not all complaints reach the Ombudsman. These are the cases that did, and three quarters were decided against the landlord.

Property condition

5,839

Property condition findings in 2024/25, up 43% from 4,092 the previous year.

Housing Ombudsman, Annual Complaints Review 2024-25

The largest category

The Ombudsman noted the rise outpaced the overall 30% increase in caseload, driven by leaks, damp, mould and heating failures, many linked to protracted repairs handling.
